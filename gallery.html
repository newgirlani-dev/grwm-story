Makeup Tips
Why is my makeup cakey even if I do my skin prep correctly?

  • Compatibility - check whether your skin prep is compatible with your makeup products. Sometimes, there are sunscreens that don't go well with certain base products making it peel off. Try changing your sunscreen or using a different base if result is still the same.
  • Powder - sometimes there are powders that don't work well with certain base formulations and/or you're using too much powder especially on areas of the face where it tends to get dry.

What’s your ‘this always saves my look’ product?

  • Whenever my makeup looks incomplete or something's missing, it's either lashes or some bronzer on my nose.

Which makeup brand do you prefer most? How much do you usually spend on makeup each month?

  • Part 1 - I have no favorite brand (this sounds very showbiz but stay with me). There are brands that really master certain makeup products. They have hero products that just really stands out. There are brands who makes really good base products, some brands carry really great lip products, etc. I'm always pro-product formulation rather than pro-brand.
  • Part 2 - Ever since becoming a content creator, very little to zero! Not because 'oh I get PR left and right' - girl I wish - but it's the fact that even as a small creator, I have way too many products to consume and I don't want to buy to waste them. I like having makeup to use, not for display.

Would a Purple Loose Powder still look good when used all over the face and if it’s okay for everyday use?

  • I personally would prefer translucent powders to set makeup if used all over the face. Other shades of loose powders like purple, yellow, pink are made for certain areas for correction because they typically have more pigment.

Makeup tips for sweaty and oily face

  • Oily - As an oily skin girl myself, let me tell you there is not going to be a magic product that's going to make you stop being oily. This is the natural state of your face and what we can do is to manage it. You can focus on oily-skin friendly layers of skincare + good combination of makeup. Makeup is not magic.
  • Sweaty - Similar above, there is not one single product that is going to solve everything. On areas of the face we move a lot like around the mouth and eyes, don't put too much product to avoid creasing and set correctly + fixing spray. To go an extra mile, you can get a face anti-perspirant used in moderation or special occassions. If we are naturally sweaty, this isn't something makeup can fix, but something we just manage.

My foundation tends to look yellow-ish to my skin, how can I fix it po? I have neutral undertone

  • First step is really making sure the shade is correct match with your undertone, second is getting a shade shifter if necessary.

As a morena, what is the hardest part in finding your shade? How do you figure out your undertone and how to figure out what shade to buy in online stores? To know if it oxidizes or if it is lightweight?

  • Undertone & Shade Match - If you find your pefect match in one try, you are one lucky person, and by saying that it means the rest of us will go through trial and error and spend money until we find the correct shade for us. It's natural, and most of the time necessary. The vein test can be unreliable, so testing it in store and in natural lighting is more effective. Visit a kiosk, swatch, buy sample sizes. A random person could give you a recommendation and still be wrong, so practice personal acumen.
  • Oxidation - This happens when the product is exposed to oxygen + the user's natural oils which means this is a case to case basis. Products could darken or it could not, there is no other way to tell than to test it.
  • Lightweight - Watch videos online or see product ingredients to know if it's silicone-based, water-based, gel-based, etc. This helps you determine how lightweight the product can go.



Content Creation
As a beauty creator, how do you balance inspiring people to try new products without making them feel like they constantly need to buy more?

  • It's all about tone & intention. Creators can be great references for products, especially when audience are not pressured to make a purchase in videos. Some contents are created to educate and that is why my PMMS Series is focusing more on teaching a skill rather than selling a product.

Any advice for aspiring creators with time, motivation, creativity block, & confidence?

  • Time & Creativity Block - I categorize myself as a Type A person - that's why I'm able to juggle jobs and hobbies at a time. I keep things organized through a productivity tool which I'm also an Ambassador of, Notion! I take down and visualize everything not just through a simple list but through a Kanban Board, Content Calendar, Eisenhower Matrix, Idea Dump Board for when I feel a spark of creativity but can't work on it yet - and most importantly, I stick by my own rules. I do Project Management for fun and run my life like the Navy girl lol!
  • Motivation - Because I already broke down the ideas that I like to work with at a certain time, it doesn't feel overwhelming! I like being able to visualize the thing I will be working on and because I've already organized it, it's much smoother and becomes easily digestible tasks. I also operate out of spite...
  • Confidence - If you can't be 100% confident right now - then just be 10% confident, or 20%, 30%, it doesn't matter. What matters is showing up despite the fear because of your conviction to loving yourself. Don't deny that person the opportunity to be great. Personify the kind of person you want to become, and think about the habits that kind of person would do, and start doing it. If you want to be a successful content creator, think like them, act like them, until you become them.

What are your struggles when you started as a content creator?

  • Equipments - You will be spending a lot of money in investing in tools. Phone, camera, lights, products etc.
  • The Valley of Despair - This is the emotional dip after starting a new hobby or job, etc. You start off extremely motivated and then comes the crashing moment when you're not seeing the results you want. It is important that you prevail. You asked for something better that's why everything is going to change.
  • Identity - Social media is saturated with creators & influencers, and if you're not convicted in what you do, why you do it, and who you do it for, you're going to wear off.

How do you get PR Packages?

  • Continue to show up! It's not just about the number of posts, followers, likes, etc., it's also the consistency of your quality & your faith in your personal brand. Brands have representatives working for them lurking around social media, and if they see you fit in the brand image, they will reach out.



General
What's the deciding factors that pushed you to quit your job and focus on content creation? And what are the challenges that comes with being an influencer or being open to the public?

  • Part 1 - I was on a sick leave and woke up around 11 AM, opened my laptop to check on some emails and messages and it felt right, it felt like it's what I should be doing, not chase after traffic and commute, KPIs and handle everything "urgent" - and so I did. I'm also juggling 4 jobs before quitting my 9-5, and then there were 3. I am still employed :D.
  • Part 2.1 - It's an agreed fact you will be battling privacy issues. I'm a small ceator so this isn't a prevailing issue on my end yet - and when I do meet people in real life, everyone is always nice and respectful!
  • Part 2.2 - When you are a public figure (public figure???), you are more prone to public scrutiny even in the little things, more often unnecessary. Although it is sad, some people on TikTok have mistaken social media as a free reign to be rude and hating just because it's "social media".

If a young girl told you she feels ugly because she doesn't look like influencers online, what would you tell her?

  • I like to put an emphasis on the parents and adults' role in supervising children on the internet, and on makeup and beauty. It's important they are guided when exploring these outlets and have an adult figure to guide them in understanding these complex ideas. The formative years of a young person are extremely important in shaping them as adults.
  • As cliche as it sounds, beauty starts from within. Security starts from within. Love starts from within. You are beautiful, no comparison, no question, no competition. Your beauty standard should be what you see in the mirror, not on your screens.

How has beauty helped you express your confidence and individuality?

  • People contain multitudes. We are complex, changing, and never just one person. Being able to change my hair, style, makeup, has helped me understand and ultimately choose the identity I want to keep being. I'm not always a decisive person, but it's in the exposure I understand what I don't want - and that made it easy for me to become who I am.

Have you ever faced pressure to have lighter skin? How did you overcome it?

  • Yes I did! It's a very common trajectory for morenas to go through a "lightening" phase for various reasons, especially in a country like ours. The thing is, no matter what I tried, they never worked so I'm very lucky nothing about my skin has changed! It is going to take years of personal growth to unlearn, but you will be very happy at the end of it. For an easier step, follow morena creators, actresses, personalities. Surround your life with people who look like you so you don't feel alienated when you see public figures who are the opposite.

Do you have insecurities?

  • Who doesn't?! I go through phases of "I could be smarter", "I could be better", "I could be prettier". I could, but I also could just be content with the way that I am and I'm still going to be happy. I am loved, I am celebrated. I love me, and I celebrate me. More is not always the better option.
